# Research brief — 2026-10-11

## 1. Hochul opts New York into Trump's school choice tax credit
- Gov. Kathy Hochul opted New York into the federal scholarship tax credit on Friday, Oct. 9, 2026; she is the second Democratic governor to opt in voluntarily, after Colorado's Jared Polis. (https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/10/09/ny-gov-hochul-opts-into-federal-scholarship-tax-credit/)
- The credit passed in 2025 as part of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, backed by President Trump. (same URL)
- From Jan. 1, taxpayers can donate up to $1,700 to a scholarship-granting organization for a dollar-for-dollar federal credit; families earning up to 300% of area median income qualify. (same URL)
- An IRS tracker shows 30 other states have opted in; GOP legislatures in Kentucky, North Carolina and Kansas overrode Democratic governors' vetoes. (same URL)
- A coalition of teachers unions and public school advocates petitioned Hochul to reject the program. Treasury and IRS issued proposed rules Oct. 1. (https://www.foxnews.com/media/gov-kathy-hochul-signs-new-york-trump-school-choice-program-despite-teachers-union-opposition)
- Union officials said they would not withdraw support for her November reelection bid. (Chalkbeat URL)

Quotes:
- Kathy Hochul: "I'm not going to leave money on the table when it could help our kids." (Fox and Chalkbeat URLs)
- Kathy Hochul: "without taking a single dollar from public schools or our state or local budgets," (Fox URL)
- Michael Mulgrew, UFT president: "is a failure of leadership," and "Gov. Hochul will bear the responsibility, but our students, educators, and families will bear the price." (Chalkbeat URL)
- New York bishops: "While such a decision is common sense from a policy perspective, it nevertheless required political courage and will." (Chalkbeat URL)
